# HungarianGP-Telemetry-Analysis

Back with more FastF1 + Python + MATLAB analysis, this time from a Hungaroring race where the data lines up almost exactly with how the afternoon actually unfolded. 

This one focused on:
📊 Driver consistency via boxplot analysis of representative lap times.
 📈 Lap time evolution via a 3-lap rolling average to track pace through the stints.

Key takeaway: Piastri's line tells the whole story on its own, very competitive pace after jumping Norris on the opening lap, before dropping off abruptly around lap 38, right where contact with a lapped Sainz began unraveling his race, just before the gearbox failure that ended it. 

Norris's numbers show a lower median but a wider spread than you'd expect from a winner, consistent with time spent managing traffic early before dropping into the low 82s once he had clean air, exactly the pace Norris said he would have given clean air in front of him.

 Antonelli quietly matched that same late pace, and secured P3, following a relatively poor qualifying from him.

Verstappen shows a brief dip right around lap 42, where he was told to ease into the new set of soft tyres, before settling into strong, stable pace on that tyre all the way to the flag, a call that helped drag a car with struggles all weekend on the podium into P2. 

Hamilton's tight, mid-pack consistency is a reminder that lap time data has limits: his race wasn't lost to pace, it was lost in the pit lane, with a speeding penalty (only 0.1km/h over) dropping him a place to his teammate. 

Hungary was a race where the midfield noise in the data had real, specific causes: penalties, strategic gambles and a blue flag system that reportedly malfunctioned for parts of the race, contributing to the very collision (Piastri and Sainz) that decided the race fight. Proof that lap-time analysis is most powerful paired with race context, not read in isolation.
